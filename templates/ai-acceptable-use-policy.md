**[Company name] — Use of AI tools policy**

**Version:** [1.0] **Effective:** [date] **Owner:** [name, role] **Review date:** [date + 12 months]

## 1. Why this policy exists

AI tools can save real time on drafting, searching and summarising. They can also disclose confidential
information in ways that are hard to undo, produce confident answers that are wrong, and record things we never
agreed to. This policy says what is allowed, what is never allowed, and who to ask.

## 2. Approved tools

Only the following tools may be used for company work, and only on the business tier, on a company account:

| Tool | Approved for | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [e.g. assistant X, business tier] | Drafting, summarising, rewriting | No client documents without [approval role] |
| [e.g. document review tool Y] | Contract and specification search | Project-scoped folders only |

Personal accounts on consumer tiers must not be used for company work, on any device.

## 3. Never put these into any AI tool

- Contracts, subcontracts and terms that are under negotiation, or marked confidential
- Anything covered by an NDA or a client's confidentiality clause
- Pricing, bid strategy, margins, or commercial terms of a live tender
- Personal data: medical and health information, disciplinary records, identity documents, payroll detail
- Site imagery of individuals where we have not given notice, and any biometric data
- [Add: anything your clients or contracts require you to keep inside company systems]

## 4. Always do these

- **Review before it leaves.** A named person reviews every output — email, letter, RFI, minutes, report —
  before it goes to a client, a subcontractor, an owner or a regulator.
- **Check anything factual.** Quantities, clause references, specification numbers, dates and figures are
  checked against the source document. If the tool cannot show where a fact came from, treat it as unverified.
- **Use the tool's own citation.** Where a tool offers citations, use them and spot check them.
- **Say when AI was used, if asked.** No one needs to volunteer it in every email, but nobody may deny it.

## 5. Specific rules for our projects

- **Drawings and documents:** upload only to [approved tool] and only within the [project folder structure].
- **Meeting minutes:** a human approves minutes line by line. A summary of a discussion is never circulated as
  a record of agreement or of a change.
- **Safety documents:** toolbox talks, JSAs and inspection records must reflect the actual task and site. A
  generated document without a person's engagement with it is not a safety record.
- **Progress and monitoring:** [describe what is monitored, what is not, and what the data will and will not
  be used for. Tell the workforce before it starts; consult as required by law or agreement.]
- **Camera and video tools:** [state retention period, who can view footage, and the process for an incident.]

## 6. What we will not do

- We will not connect an AI tool to a system with write access to our accounting, payroll, scheduling or ERP
  systems.
- We will not use AI output as the sole basis for a decision about a person — hiring, discipline, or
  performance assessment.
- We will not use AI to make a decision on a safety matter without a competent person involved.
- We will not use face recognition or biometric identification of workers. [Remove only with legal advice.]

## 7. If something goes wrong

Tell [role, contact] immediately if you think confidential information has been shared with a tool, if an
output turned out to be wrong after it left the company, or if you are unsure whether something is allowed.
Reporting quickly is the right behaviour and will not be treated as a disciplinary matter. [Adjust with HR /
legal advice for your jurisdiction.]
