# Progress tracking

**Answers the question:** what is actually installed, right now, and where does that differ from what the
schedule says should be?

**Maturity:** usable on the right jobs — those with a maintained model, a capture habit, and someone who acts
on the difference.

**Data it needs:** 360° photos or scans taken on a cadence, an up-to-date model or drawing set, a schedule
updated weekly, and consistent location names between all three.

## What it actually does

- **Capture.** A 360° camera walk or a laser scan, weekly or fortnightly, producing a dated, navigable record
  of the site.
- **Comparison.** Capture is compared against the model or drawing set to estimate what is physically
  installed, by area or by level.
- **Percent complete.** Installed quantities or areas roll up to progress — used for the weekly update, for
  the owner's meeting, or as an input to earned value.
- **Deviation lists.** The genuinely valuable output: what is missing from the model, what is installed where
  the model says it isn't, and where a clash or rework is visible before it is buried.

The capture itself is often worth the money before any AI touches it: a dated, walkable record of every week
of the job is unusually strong evidence in a delay or backcharge argument. It is also evidence that cuts both
ways — it can just as easily show your own delay, or an unsafe condition nobody acted on. Decide retention and
disclosure with your lawyer before the first walk, and see
[risks and controls](../04-risks-and-controls.md).

## What it needs from you

- **A current model or drawing set.** Comparison against a design nobody built is worse than no comparison,
  because it produces confident wrong answers and arguments with your trade partners.
- **A capture owner and a cadence.** Someone walks the job every week whether or not anyone asked. If this
  depends on enthusiasm, it stops in week three.
- **Consistent locations.** The same area names must exist in the model, the schedule and the capture —
  "Level 2 east" in three systems is not one location.
- **Schedule discipline.** Weekly updates, or progress data has nothing to be compared against.
- **Trade cooperation, agreed up front.** If a subcontractor discovers it is being measured via a report,
  the first conversation is an argument rather than a correction.

## Where it goes wrong

- **Capture without consequence.** Photographs pile up in a viewer nobody opens. The technology works; the
  process was never wired to a decision. Fix it by naming the person who reads the deviation list on Monday.
- **Progress percentage fights.** "Percent complete" is a contractual term, and the subcontract usually
  defines the method — installed quantities, value of work in place, or milestones. If your tool computes it
  a different way, you will lose the argument, correctly.
- **Progress is not payment.** Work installed is not work accepted, approved, or billable. Never let a
  progress percentage reach a pay application without the contractual check.
- **Model drift.** The design changes; the model does not. Comparing weekly against a stale model burns
  credibility fast — the crew will tell you, loudly, the second time it happens.
- **Cost and disruption of scanning.** Full scans are slower and need planning around the trades. Match the
  method to the question: photos for visual progress, scans where you need measurement.

## How to judge a pilot

Run it as a six-week comparison rather than a demo:

1. Pick **two areas** with a known, busy trade sequence.
2. Capture weekly. Alongside it, ask the superintendent to write down their own view of percent complete per
   area, before seeing the tool's number.
3. Compare weekly. On disagreements, go and look. Record **who was right**, and how long it took to find out.
4. Measure three things: **time saved** on the weekly update, **lead time** on the first deviation that
   actually mattered (how many days before someone would otherwise have found it), and **adoption** — did the
   super look at week six the way they looked at week one?

The last point is the honest one. If the tool is accurate and nobody opens it, you have bought a very
expensive camera.

## Questions worth asking a vendor

- Does it need a BIM model, or will it work from a 2D drawing set — or from photos alone?
- How is percent complete computed: areas, quantities, cost, or the schedule's own method? Can I choose?
- Can the deviation list be exported per area with a photo and a location, into a job I can assign?
- Which scheduling systems do you read and write (P6, MS Project, others), and one-way or both?
- What capture hardware, and how long does a 10,000 m² floor take to walk with it?
- Who processes the imagery, where, and is any of it used for training?
- How do you handle a design change or a reissued model mid-job?
- What happens to my capture history if I stop paying?
